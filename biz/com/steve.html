A Notice to the Curious

Each bottle is filled in a small room with one window and a humming refrigerator.

Steve does not believe in batches. He believes in moods. The moods are bottled when they arrive, and labeled with whatever he was thinking about at the time — a streetlight, a dog he saw once, the smell of rain on warm asphalt at 3 a.m.

If something is sold out, it means the mood has passed. It may return. It may not. This is part of the arrangement.

— posted on the door, in pencil

§ II

Concerning Steve

Steve learned to distill from a man who ran a gas station in northern Michigan and kept an alembic in the back room next to a shelf of expired motor oil. The man's name was also Steve, which Steve has always found suspicious but also, he says, "fitting."

He works alone. He listens to the same three records over and over: one is in Polish. He believes that smell is the only sense that tells the truth, and that everything else is mostly persuasion.

Each item is made in small numbers because the room is small and because Steve tires easily. If a thing is here, he made it this season. If a thing is gone, it is gone for the time being.

HOUSE RULES, posted above the sink:
  • do not open the green jar
  • the still is named Margaret
  • shipping takes 5–9 days, give or take a Sunday
  • returns are accepted but rarely necessary
  • if it smells wrong, it isn't wrong, it is for someone else

§ IV

How to Order

The Procedure

  1. Place items in your basket up there ↗
  2. When ready, press Send to Steve
  3. Your order opens an email — Steve reads it that evening, usually with tea
  4. He'll reply within a day with payment instructions & shipping confirmation
  5. Bottles go out in brown paper. Sometimes there is a note. Sometimes there is a small drawing.

No accounts. No subscriptions. No algorithm. Just Steve, an inbox, and a pencil.
